Probabilistic Stock Price Forecasting

M.Sc. thesis results: Quantile LSTM, FinBERT sentiment, walk-forward validation

"A Hybrid Deep Learning Framework for Stock Price Prediction Integrating Quantile Regression and Sentiment Analysis", IU International University of Applied Sciences

Summary

  • The model forecasts a range (10th, 50th and 90th percentile) instead of a single price. The 80% interval was close to its target at 1 day, with 78.8% to 83.0% coverage.
  • Price error was about the same as a naive "tomorrow equals today" forecast, which is expected for large-cap stocks. The value of the model is the calibrated interval and the directional signal, not a lower price error.
  • Sentiment from news changed the forecasts, but did not consistently reduce error. It helped most at 10 days and on days with news events.
  • The 2022 rate-rise year was the hardest test: directional accuracy fell below 50% for all three stocks.

Setup

Three stocks with different volatility (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA), 2,844 daily observations each (January 2015 to April 2026). Five-fold walk-forward validation with an expanding training window, tested on 2021 to 2025, so every result is out-of-sample. Horizons: 1, 5 and 10 trading days.

The XGBoost quantile baseline, trained on the same features, covered the real price only 70.1% of the time at 1 day, so the sequential LSTM gives noticeably better calibrated intervals. Against the naive random-walk forecast, price error was within about 0.1 percentage points in every case (thesis Table 4-8).

Where Sentiment Helps

Across all days, adding sentiment did not reduce price error. Splitting AAPL's 10-day forecasts by market condition shows the effect is conditional:

Source: thesis Table 4-12. Event days are a small subset, so the 72.2% figure is indicative, not a guarantee.

Paired t-tests showed sentiment shifted the median forecast significantly in six of nine stock-horizon combinations (p < 0.05).

Limitations

  • Regime change: in the 2022 test year, directional accuracy fell below 50% for all three stocks (45.4%, 47.4%, 48.2%). The model had never seen a rising-rate period, and it recovered once 2022 entered the training data.
  • News coverage: only about 24% to 27% of trading days had any news, which dilutes the average effect of sentiment.
  • Interval calibration: at 10 days, coverage fell short of 80% for NVDA and MSFT.
  • Scope: three large-cap US technology stocks, no macroeconomic inputs, and no formal backtest with trading costs. The results describe statistical forecasting performance, not trading profit.
  • Sentiment validation: the sentiment model was validated on Financial PhraseBank, not on a labelled sample of the actual news articles used.
