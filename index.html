Projects

Probabilistic Stock Price Forecasting

M.Sc. thesis, IU International University of Applied Sciences: "A Hybrid Deep Learning Framework for Stock Price Prediction Integrating Quantile Regression and Sentiment Analysis"

Problem: Most stock forecasting models output a single number and say nothing about uncertainty. This project forecasts a range (10th, 50th and 90th percentile of future returns) for AAPL, MSFT and NVDA at horizons from 1 to 10 trading days.

Approach: A two-layer Quantile LSTM, combined with news sentiment from a FinBERT model fine-tuned with LoRA. Evaluated with five-fold walk-forward validation and compared against an XGBoost quantile regression baseline.

Data: Daily prices from Yahoo Finance (January 2015 to April 2026, 2,844 trading days per stock), about 1,200 to 1,350 news articles per stock from Alpha Vantage, GDELT and Yahoo Finance News, and the Financial PhraseBank dataset for the sentiment model.

Tools: Python, TensorFlow, XGBoost, FinBERT, LoRA, yfinance

Results:

  • Prediction intervals were close to their 80% target at 1 day (78.8% to 83.0% coverage), versus 70.1% for the XGBoost baseline.
  • At 10 days, coverage ranged from 70.9% (NVDA) to 80.1% (AAPL), versus 65.1% for the baseline.
  • Fine-tuning FinBERT with LoRA raised macro F1 from 0.31 (zero-shot) to 0.80.
  • Sentiment did not consistently reduce price error, but on news event days directional accuracy for AAPL at 10 days reached 72.2% (58.2% overall).
  • Price error was comparable to a naive random-walk baseline, as expected for large-cap stocks.

Relevance: An end-to-end analytics workflow: collecting and cleaning data from several sources, feature engineering, honest validation, and communicating uncertainty instead of a single number.

Product Analytics: Retention, Funnel and A/B Testing

Portfolio project on a simulated European mobility platform (synthetic data)

Problem: Where do users drop out of the booking funnel, which acquisition channels bring the best users, and does a simplified booking flow improve conversion?

Approach: SQL analysis on a SQLite database, six Python notebooks covering funnel, D1/D7/D30 retention, cohorts and segmentation, an A/B test evaluated with significance testing and 95% confidence intervals, and a multi-page Power BI dashboard.

Data: Synthetic dataset generated for this project: 5,000 users, 141,813 events and 2,731 completed bookings (January 2024 to June 2025), checked with 23 validation checks and 25 automated tests.

Tools: Python, SQL, SQLite, Pandas, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Power BI, Pytest, Git

Results (on synthetic data, so they demonstrate the method rather than real business outcomes):

  • Referral users converted best (about 40%) versus Paid Search (about 23.5%).
  • Web conversion (about 24%) trailed iOS (about 30%) and Android (about 28.8%), pointing to the Web booking journey for review.
  • The simplified booking flow lifted conversion from 26.9% to 30.5% (+3.6 percentage points).
  • Feature adoption was linked to longer engagement, but the relationship is observational, not causal.

Relevance: The full analyst workflow in one project: framing business questions, SQL, exploratory analysis, statistical testing, dashboarding and recommendations.
